Employment-Based Adjustment of Status Interview

Many employment-based I-485 cases are decided without an interview, but USCIS may schedule one. The officer confirms identity, admissibility and that the job offer and qualifications match the petition.

Download the free PDF guide (47 KB)

Key facts from official sources

  • Employment-based immigrant categories generally require an approved or concurrently filed immigrant petition such as Form I-140. [source 2]
  • Form I-485 is used to adjust status to permanent residence; Form I-765 can be filed for work authorization while pending. [source 1]

Commonly reported practice questions

Commonly reported practice questions: these are general topics people report being asked about. They are NOT an official USCIS or Department of State list. Officers may ask anything related to your case. Answer truthfully and consistently with your forms.

  • Your job title, duties, employer and work location.
  • Your education and experience as listed in the petition.
  • Your immigration history and maintenance of status.
  • Your intent to work in the sponsored position.
  • Questions confirming answers on your I-485.

Document checklist

Follow your interview notice or embassy instructions first.

  • Interview notice
  • Passport, I-94 and status documents (e.g., visa approvals, I-20s, EADs)
  • I-140 approval or receipt notice
  • Recent employment verification letter on company letterhead
  • Recent pay stubs and tax records
  • Diplomas, transcripts and experience letters
  • Medical exam (I-693) if requested

Interview day tips

  • Know your job description in the petition well.
  • Bring evidence of status maintenance for each period in the U.S.
  • If you changed employers while pending, speak with your attorney before the interview.
