Naturalization (N-400) Interview & 2025 Civics Test

The naturalization interview is where a USCIS officer reviews your Form N-400 with you under oath and gives you the English and civics tests.

Download the free PDF guide (79 KB)

Key facts from official sources

  • If you filed Form N-400 on or after Oct. 20, 2025, you take the 2025 civics test: an oral test of 20 questions from the list of 128. You must answer 12 correctly to pass, and you fail if you answer 9 incorrectly. [source 1]
  • If you filed before Oct. 20, 2025, the 2008 test applies: up to 10 questions from a list of 100; 6 correct answers pass. [source 1]
  • English reading: read aloud one out of three sentences correctly. English writing: write one out of three sentences correctly. Speaking is judged by the officer during the interview. [source 1]
  • You get two attempts to pass the English and civics tests. [source 1]
  • 65/20 special consideration: if you are 65 or older and have been a permanent resident for 20+ years, you may study only the 20 starred questions, may take the civics test in your language, are asked 10 of them and must answer 6 correctly. [source 2]
  • Some answers change with elections or appointments; answer with the official serving at the time of your interview and check uscis.gov/citizenship/testupdates. [source 2]

Commonly reported practice questions

Commonly reported practice questions: these are general topics people report being asked about. They are NOT an official USCIS or Department of State list. Officers may ask anything related to your case. Answer truthfully and consistently with your forms.

  • Questions confirming each answer on your N-400 (addresses, employment, travel outside the U.S., marital history, children).
  • The 'Have you ever...' eligibility questions in Part 9 of the N-400, and that you understand what they mean.
  • Questions about your willingness to take the Oath of Allegiance.
  • Simple questions to check that you understand spoken English, such as about your day, your work or how you got to the interview.

Document checklist

Follow your interview notice or embassy instructions first.

  • Your interview notice (and arrive early)
  • Permanent Resident Card (green card)
  • Government-issued photo ID
  • Passports and travel documents, current and expired, covering your residence period
  • Copy of the N-400 you filed (review it the night before)
  • Any documents listed on your interview notice (e.g., marriage/divorce records, tax transcripts, court records)
  • Study materials: the 128 questions below and the reading/writing vocabulary lists

Interview day tips

  • Study answers out loud; the civics test is oral.
  • Practice reading and writing short civics sentences from the official vocabulary lists.
  • If you do not understand a question, it is fine to ask the officer to repeat or rephrase it.
  • Never guess about a 'Have you ever' question; if something applies, talk to an attorney before the interview.

B: System of Government

  1. Name the three branches of government.
    • Legislative, executive, and judicial

  14. Name your U.S. representative.

    • People from their (congressional) district
    • People in their district
  19. Who elects members of the House of Representatives?
    • Citizens from their (congressional) district
  20. Some states have more representatives than other states. Why?
    • (Because of) the state’s population
    • (Because) they have more people
    • (Because) some states have more people
  21. The President of the United States is elected for how many years? ★
    • Four (4) years
  22. The President of the United States can serve only two terms. Why?

  24. What is the name of the Vice President of the United States now? ★
    • Visit uscis.gov/citizenship/testupdates for the name of the Vice President of the United States.
  25. If the president can no longer serve, who becomes president?

  26. Name one power of the president.

    • Vetoes bills
    • Enforces laws
    • Commander in Chief (of the military)
    • Chief diplomat
    • Appoints federal judges
  27. Who is Commander in Chief of the U.S. military?
    • The President (of the United States)
  28. Who signs bills to become laws?
    • The President (of the United States)
  29. Who vetoes bills? ★
    • The President (of the United States)
  30. Who appoints federal judges?
    • The President (of the United States)
  31. The executive branch has many parts. Name one.
    • President (of the United States)

  32. What does the President’s Cabinet do?

    • Director of the Central Intelligence Agency

    • Director of National Intelligence
    • United States Trade Representative
  34. Why is the Electoral College important?
    • It decides who is elected president.

  35. What is one part of the judicial branch?
    • Supreme Court
    • Federal Courts
  36. What does the judicial branch do?
    • Reviews laws

    • Resolves disputes (disagreements) about the law

  37. What is the highest court in the United States? ★

  2. Who can vote in federal elections, run for federal office, and serve on a jury in the United States?

    • Be loyal to the United States
