Removal of Conditions (I-751) Interview

If you received a 2-year conditional green card through marriage, you file Form I-751 to remove the conditions. USCIS may decide without an interview or may schedule one.

Download the free PDF guide (46 KB)

Key facts from official sources

  • Conditional residents through marriage filing jointly must file Form I-751 during the 90-day period immediately before conditional residence expires. [source 1]
  • Some people may request a waiver of the joint filing requirement, for example after divorce or in cases of battery or extreme cruelty. [source 1]

Commonly reported practice questions

Commonly reported practice questions: these are general topics people report being asked about. They are NOT an official USCIS or Department of State list. Officers may ask anything related to your case. Answer truthfully and consistently with your forms.

  • What your life together has looked like since you got the conditional card.
  • Changes in address, work or household.
  • Shared finances and responsibilities.
  • Children born or adopted during the marriage, if any.
  • If filing with a waiver: the circumstances described in your petition.

Document checklist

Follow your interview notice or embassy instructions first.

  • Interview notice
  • Conditional green card and IDs
  • Evidence of an ongoing shared life since the card was issued: joint tax returns, lease/mortgage, bank statements, insurance, children's birth certificates
  • Copy of your I-751 and receipt notice
  • Any court or divorce records if applicable

Interview day tips

  • Focus your evidence on the time period since you received the conditional card.
  • Keep the receipt notice; it extends your status while the case is pending.
  • If your situation changed (separation, divorce, abuse), talk to an attorney about waiver options.
